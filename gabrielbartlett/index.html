The team's current build is an electric hydroplane for the American Society of Naval Engineers' Promoting Electric Propulsion competition. The race exists to speed up research into electric propulsion in an industry that still runs on gas.

As president, my goal for this year is to put members on real engineering work while they're still in school: hulls, powertrains, and the failures in between.

Seadog II is the 2026 version of our race boat, built on a donated 8.5 ft plywood hydroplane hull. I led hull design across two seasons on it: modeling modifications in SolidWorks, cutting out and replacing hull sections, and refinishing it from bare wood in epoxy and paint each year.

The first version, Seadog I, got the electric powertrain integrated into the hull and took first place at UConn's ECE Demo Day in 2025.

For 2026 I joined the battery team spot welding a custom 13S56P lithium-ion pack: 728 18650 cells, built to stay inside the 55.5 V race limit. Then we took Seadog II to ASNE's Promoting Electric Propulsion competition, where most of race weekend was spent in the pits with the hatches open.

On Big Dawg I, I led the hull design team: taking the hull from concept to a shape the team could build, and keeping the group coordinated while we did it.

The hull is being modeled in SolidWorks: a long, narrow forward hull with a raised canopy, and a pair of wings that hang below it on either side. The CAD comes first so the team can check the shape before any material gets cut.

Copper is a small cat robot that keeps you on task while you study. I'm building it to get hands-on with electronic components and to design and 3D print the body in SolidWorks.

The body is a printed base plate with four hip servos and four leg assemblies, eight micro servos in total. They all run off a PCA9685 servo driver talking to an Arduino over I²C, with separate DC power for the servos. The firmware takes text commands over serial, so I can test each move from the laptop before it walks on its own.

The long-term goal is to design a small-scale custom battery for it.
